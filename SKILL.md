# AI Writing Tropes to Avoid

Source: [tropes.fyi](https://tropes.fyi) by [ossama.is](https://ossama.is).

Any one of these used once can be fine. The tell is several together, or one repeated. Write varied, imperfect, specific prose.

## Word choice

- **Magic adverbs.** "Quietly", "deeply", "fundamentally", "remarkably", "arguably" used to make mundane things feel significant. *"quietly orchestrating workflows"*
- **"Delve" and friends.** "Delve", "certainly", "utilize", "leverage" (verb), "robust", "streamline", "harness".
- **Grandiose nouns.** "Tapestry", "landscape", "paradigm", "synergy", "ecosystem", "framework" where a plain word works. *"the ever-evolving landscape of technology"*
- **"Serves as" dodge.** "Serves as", "stands as", "marks", "represents" in place of "is". *"The building serves as a reminder of..."*

## Sentence structure

- **Negative parallelism.** "It's not X, it's Y", the most common AI tell. Includes "not because X, but because Y", "X, not Y", and "The question isn't X. The question is Y." *"It's not bold. It's backwards."*
- **"Not X. Not Y. Just Z."** A countdown of negations before the point. *"Not a bug. Not a feature. A design flaw."*
- **"The X? A Y."** A question nobody asked, answered right away. *"The result? Devastating."*
- **Anaphora.** The same opening repeated across consecutive sentences. *"They assume... They assume... They assume..."*
- **Tricolon stacking.** Rule-of-three lists back to back, or stretched to four or five.
- **Filler transitions.** "It's worth noting", "it bears mentioning", "importantly", "interestingly", "notably".
- **Participle analysis.** A trailing "-ing" clause that adds fake significance. *"...highlighting its enduring legacy"*
- **False ranges.** "From X to Y" where X and Y sit on no real scale. *"From innovation to cultural transformation."*

## Paragraph structure

- **Punchy fragments.** Short sentences as standalone paragraphs for manufactured emphasis. *"He published this. Openly. In a book."*
- **Listicle in a trench coat.** A list disguised as prose: "The first... The second... The third..."

## Tone

- **False suspense.** "Here's the kicker", "here's the thing", "here's where it gets interesting", "here's the deal".
- **Patronizing analogy.** "Think of it as...", "It's like a..." when the plain concept was already clear.
- **"Imagine a world where..."** Futurism used to sell the argument.
- **False vulnerability.** Polished, risk-free self-awareness. *"And yes, I'm openly in love with the platform model."*
- **"The truth is simple."** Asserting clarity instead of showing it, or "the real story is..." as a privileged reveal.
- **Stakes inflation.** Every topic becomes world-historical. *"will define the next era of computing"*
- **"Let's break this down."** Teacher voice: "let's unpack", "let's explore", "let's dive in".
- **Vague attributions.** "Experts argue", "industry reports suggest", or one source inflated into "several publications".
- **Invented concept labels.** Domain word plus abstract noun (paradox, trap, creep, divide) used as if it were an established term. *"the acceleration trap"*

## Formatting

- **Em-dash addiction.** A human uses two or three per piece; AI uses twenty.
- **Bold-first bullets.** Every list item opens with a bolded phrase.
- **Unicode decoration.** Arrows (→) and curly quotes a person wouldn't type on a keyboard.

## Composition

- **Fractal summaries.** Every section previews and recaps itself, and so does the document.
- **Dead metaphor.** One metaphor repeated five to ten times.
- **Historical analogy stacking.** Rapid-fire company or tech-era examples to borrow authority. *"Apple didn't build Uber. Facebook didn't build Spotify..."*
- **One-point dilution.** One argument restated many ways to look comprehensive.
- **Content duplication.** The same paragraph appearing twice.
- **Signposted conclusion.** "In conclusion", "to sum up", "in summary".
- **"Despite its challenges..."** Acknowledge a problem only to dismiss it with optimism.
